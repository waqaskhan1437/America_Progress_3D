### SCENE 3: Revolutionary War (Frame 61-90)
**Visual:** Declaration of Independence document, battle scenes
**Narration:**

### SCENE 4: Louisiana Purchase (Frame 91-120)
**Visual:** Map showing territory doubling, Jefferson meeting
**Narration:**
"In 1803, Thomas Jefferson doubled the nation's size with the Louisiana Purchase. For just 15 million dollars, America acquired 828,000 square miles of territory."

### SCENE 5: Westward Expansion (Frame 121-150)
**Visual:** Covered wagons, trails, frontier settlements
**Narration:**
"Manifest Destiny drove Americans westward. The Oregon Trail, the Santa Fe Trail... thousands of families seeking a new life in the West."

### SCENE 6: Civil War (Frame 151-180)
**Visual:** Split nation, North vs South visualization
**Narration:**
"But division came. The Civil War (1861-1865) tested the nation. Over 600,000 lives lost, but the Union prevailed. America was one again."

## CHAPTER 3: INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION (1865-1900)

### SCENE 7: Industrial Awakening (Frame 181-210)
**Visual:** Factories, railroads connecting the nation, steel mills
**Narration:**
"After the war, America transformed. The Industrial Revolution brought factories, steel, and railroads. By 1900, America was the world's largest industrial power."

### SCENE 8: Immigration Wave (Frame 211-240)
**Visual:** Ships arriving at Ellis Island, diverse crowds
**Narration:**
"Between 1860 and 1920, over 30 million immigrants arrived. Irish, Italians, Germans, Jews, Chinese... they came seeking freedom and opportunity. They built America."

### SCENE 9: City Transformation - OLD vs NEW (Frame 241-270)
**Visual:** 
- Left side: Old colonial city (Philadelphia 1800s - small buildings, dirt streets)
- Right side: Modern American city (New York 1900 - skyscrapers, electric streetcars)

"Cities transformed. What was once a small colonial town became a metropolis. New York, Chicago, Detroit - these cities roared with life and ambition."
